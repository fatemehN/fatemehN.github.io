Fatemeh N. Nokabadi

Machine Learning Scientist | Computer Vision, VLMs & Video Understanding

I am a Machine Learning Scientist with over a decade of experience in computer vision and machine learning. I hold a Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from Université Laval, where I conducted my doctoral research in collaboration with Mila – Quebec AI Institute, under the supervision of Prof. Christian Gagné and Prof. Jean-François Lalonde.

My work spans video understanding, object detection and tracking, model robustness, and multimodal vision-language models. I enjoy bridging research and practical applications, developing models and learning systems that are both effective and reliable.

Currently, I work at Employment and Social Development Canada, focusing on multimodal AI and document intelligence, including fine-tuning vision-language and recognition models for privacy-sensitive applications.

My research has been published in venues including TMLR and CRV, and presented at NeurIPS and ICML. I am particularly interested in advancing applied AI, exploring emerging technologies, and turning challenging research problems into practical solutions.

Robustness Evaluation of Vision Models

TMLR 2024CRV 2025NeurIPS 2024Adversarial MLObject TrackingVision Transformers
  • Stress-tested state-of-the-art transformer trackers (MixFormer, OSTrack, TransT-M, ROMTrack, RTS) on GOT-10k, VOT2022 and UAV123 with white-box attacks that operate through predicted bounding boxes and segmentation masks.
  • Proposed ABBG, a single-bounding-box attack that remains effective across transformer trackers where prior methods fail.
  • Introduced TrackPGD, a white-box attack built on an object-binary-mask proxy that applies to mask-based trackers earlier attacks could not target.
  • Led a systematic reproducibility study across attacks, tracker architectures and perturbation budgets, awarded the TMLR Reproducibility Certification.
  • Investigated diffusion-based adversarial purification for video, characterizing the temporal-consistency limits of frame-wise approaches.

Representation Learning for Video Understanding

WiML @ ICML 2024Representation LearningReinforcement LearningVideo Tracking
  • Framed single-object tracking as a reinforcement-learning episodic task with a Soft-Actor-Critic agent learning a motion policy from video frames.
  • Proposed the β-Multivariational Autoencoder (βMVAE) and a U-Net variant (βMVUNet) to learn an entangled Multivariate Gaussian prior (mean vector and covariance matrix) over the tracker's correlated motion parameters directly from frame patches.
  • Jointly trained the decoder to predict the target's binary segmentation mask, so a single network learns the motion distribution and the per-frame object mask used by the tracker.
  • Avoided the posterior collapse that flow-based baselines (RealNVP, IAF, planar and radial flows) showed on the same data.
  • Curated a custom single-object tracking dataset derived from YouTube-VOS.
